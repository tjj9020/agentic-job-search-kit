### Mis-ships and the pack-station scan
Role / employer / dates: Operations Manager, Northfield Freight, 2022
Situation: 2.1% of orders shipped with the wrong item or quantity. The largest retail customer had put the account on a 90-day review. Errors were caught, when they were caught, by a QA sampler at the dock after the box was sealed.
Behavior: Pulled 400 error tickets and sorted them by where the mistake happened; 71% happened at pack, not pick. Rejected the proposal to add four QA samplers. Put a scan-to-verify step at each pack station so the box could not be labeled until every item matched the order. Piloted on one line for three weeks before rolling out.
Impact: Mis-ship rate 2.1% to 0.6% in nine months. Customer ended the review. Pack rate dipped 6% in the first month and recovered by month three.
Proof level: hard number (monthly quality report)
Skills / tags: quality, root cause, process design, customer retention, lean
Candidate's own words: "we were inspecting mistakes after we'd already taped them shut"; "I didn't need more checkers, I needed fewer mistakes"

### Peak season: late shipments
Role / employer / dates: Operations Manager, Northfield Freight, 2023 to 2024
Situation: In the 2023 peak, 11% of orders shipped late. The building ran mandatory overtime for six weeks and still fell behind. Fast-moving items were slotted at the back of the building because that was where space had been free in the spring.
Behavior: Re-slotted the top 200 items to the front in September using the prior year's order data. Cross-trained 30 associates across pick, pack and receiving so labor could move to the bottleneck each day. Set a daily 2 p.m. check against the plan with shift leads.
Impact: 2024 peak late shipments 3% of orders. Overtime hours down 22% against 2023 on 9% more volume.
Proof level: hard number
Skills / tags: peak planning, labor planning, slotting, cross-training
Candidate's own words: "we stopped guessing at 2 o'clock"; "the fast stuff was parked at the back"
