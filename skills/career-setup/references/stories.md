# Their stories

A story is one accomplishment told so that it can prove a job requirement and survive an interview question. Invite the user to share anything that documents their work (reviews, write-ups, reports); most won't have any, and conversation is enough.

Save each one in `resume-story-bank.md` like this:

```
### Short story name
Role / employer / dates:
Situation: what was broken, missing or at risk, in concrete terms
Behavior: what they personally did and decided, including options they rejected
Impact: the result, with numbers and before-and-after where they exist
Proof level: hard number, estimate, or qualitative
Skills / tags: the kinds of job requirements this story proves
Candidate's own words: phrases they actually said or wrote, quoted exactly
```

## Running the conversation

Start one story per resume bullet, leaving unknown fields blank, and begin with the ones that matter most for the jobs they want. Take one story at a time and one question per turn. The open questions saved earlier belong to these stories; ask each when you reach its story, as a short plain question with an example of a good answer:

> Do you remember roughly how many falls the unit had in a year, before you changed anything? Something like "maybe 12, down to about 5" is plenty.

What turns a resume line into a story is usually one of these, so listen for them and ask when they are missing:

- what was actually going wrong before they acted
- the decision that was theirs, not the team's
- what they chose not to do, and why
- the number, or an honest estimate marked as one
- who would vouch for it

When they say something in a vivid or plain way, save the exact phrase under "Candidate's own words". Those phrases are the raw material for their voice.

If they ask you to make something sound bigger than it was, record what is true and say why: it will be asked about, and it can be checked.

## Rounding out

Once the resume is covered, see what kinds of story are missing, fitted to their field and level: something that went wrong and what changed after, getting money or people for something, saying no, helping someone grow, a hard trade-off, getting a result without authority. Ask for the ones that would matter for the jobs they want. It is fine if they have none.

End the document with "Open questions" (numbered), "Story gaps" and "Tailored versions" (empty for now).
