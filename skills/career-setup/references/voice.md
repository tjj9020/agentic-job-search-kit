# Their voice

The goal is a short profile that lets you write lines the user would recognize as their own. Recruiters discard resumes that sound machine-made, and the user has to be able to say every line out loud.

Save it as `resume-voice-profile.md`. Read the "Candidate's own words" lines in the story bank first.

## What to learn from

Ask for three to five things they wrote themselves, and say what counts: work emails, a report, feedback on a colleague, a long text message, a post. Writing from before they used AI is best. For each, ask whether they wrote it alone; leave out anything AI-assisted as a voice sample (its facts are still usable), because otherwise you learn the AI's habits and hand them back. If they have nothing, ask them to type or dictate three short answers and use those.

## Building the profile

1. **What they actually do.** Write 8 to 12 observations, each with a quoted example from their own text: how they open, where the numbers go, whether they name the specific thing or abstract it, verbs, adjectives, sentence length, how they credit others, how candid they are. Describe their habits, not good writing.
2. **Style picks.** Take one real accomplishment and offer it two ways, one pair at a time, asking which sounds like them: plain verbs or elevated ones; what was wrong first or the result first; dry or warm; a summary that opens with the problem they fix or with their title.
3. **Talk it out.** Ask them to describe two or three accomplishments the way they would to a peer. Keep the phrases worth keeping.
4. **Words.** Start a banned list with common resume filler (spearheaded, leveraged, orchestrated, championed, synergy, robust, seamless, cutting-edge, passionate, results-driven, proven track record, transformative, innovative). Ask what else they would never say and what they do say. The test: would they say it out loud to a peer?
5. **Gold examples.** Write six resume bullets and one summary from their stories. They approve, edit or reject each. Keep the approved ones, and adjust your observations to match their edits.
6. **Notes on their current resume.** Say where it doesn't sound like them and what makes it read as templated.

Give the profile a version and a status line. It is approved only when they say so. After that, every time they edit a line you wrote, update the profile with what the edit shows.
