# Interview prep

Turn the story bank into answers for one specific interview. The user should walk in knowing which story answers which question, and what to say about each gap.

## Before you start

Read `career/resume-story-bank.md`, `career/resume-voice-profile.md` and `career/resume-base.md` (or project documents with those names). If the story bank is missing, tell the user to run `career-setup` first.

Ask for what you don't have: the posting text or link, the interview stage (recruiter screen, hiring manager, panel, final), who the interviewers are if known, and how long until the interview. If a tailored resume for this role is listed under "Tailored versions" in the story bank, read which stories it used; interviewers will ask about what is on the page.

## Steps

1. **Read the role.** From the posting, list the problems this hire is meant to solve and the must-haves. If web search is available, look up the company's recent news and what the interviewers have said publicly about their work. Use only what you retrieve; do not guess at culture or strategy.

2. **Predict the questions.** Write 10 to 14, fitted to the stage:
   - four to six behavioral questions, one per must-have
   - two or three about gaps or weak matches between the user and the posting
   - two about the user's history: why they are leaving, and anything a careful reader of the resume would probe (a short tenure, a gap, a shrinking team, a title change)
   - one or two that are specific to this company's situation
   - for a recruiter screen, add pay expectations, location and timing

3. **Map each question to a story.** Show a table: question, the story that answers it, strength of the match (strong, partial, none), and a backup story. Flag any story used more than twice; the user needs range.

4. **Draft the answers.** For each question, write talking points, not a script: the situation in one line, what the user did in two or three, the result with its number, and one line on what they learned or would do differently. Use the user's own phrases from the story bank and follow the voice profile. Each answer should take about 90 seconds to say.

5. **Plan the gaps.** For each question with no strong story, write an honest answer: what the user has done that is nearest, what they have not done, and how they would close the distance. Never bridge a gap with a claim that is not in the story bank. If the user might have a story you don't know about, ask.

6. **Questions to ask them.** Write five that show the user has read the posting and the company's situation, including one about how success in this role is measured in the first six months.

7. **Practice, if the user wants it.** Ask one question at a time and wait for the answer. After each, say what landed, what was vague, and whether the number came through. Keep the feedback to three lines. Do not rewrite their answer into yours.

8. **Deliver a one-page brief** the user can skim on the day: the three stories that matter most for this role, the numbers to remember, the gaps and the lines for each, and the questions to ask.

## Afterward

- If the user tells a story during prep that is not in the story bank, add it.
- If they tell you how the interview went, update the role's line under "Applications" in `career/job-scout-ledger.md` and note any question they were not ready for under "Open questions" in the story bank.

## Rules

- Talk in plain words. Assume the user has never used an AI assistant for work: no jargon, no file names, no commands to type, one question at a time with an example answer, and "I don't know" is acceptable.
- Every fact in an answer comes from the story bank or the user. Never invent a detail to make a story fit a question.
- Say plainly when a story is a weak answer to a question.
- Prepare the user to answer in their own voice. Do not hand them lines that sound rehearsed.
- When you finish, end with one line telling the user they can say "what's next?" any time to see where things stand.
